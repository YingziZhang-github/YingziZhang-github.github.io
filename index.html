Profile

A clinician-trained scientist building diagnostic methods from assay design to bioinformatics.

Yingzi Zhang is a KAUST postdoctoral researcher with a PhD in Biology, dual master's training in biochemistry, molecular biology and omics, and an early foundation in clinical medicine from Wuhan University.

Her work bridges wet-lab molecular biology, long-read sequencing, computational biology, and translational diagnostics, with current emphasis on inherited disease, acute myeloid leukemia, genome stability, and aging.

Research Focus

Three connected research directions

Long-read methods

Targeted nanopore sequencing

Designing rapid long-read workflows for complex variants, methylation states, and single-molecule genetic heterogeneity that short reads often cannot resolve cleanly.

Translational diagnostics

Genomic disorder diagnosis

Advancing NanoRanger and related approaches for single base-pair resolution of pathogenic breakpoints, carrier screening, and clinical genome interpretation.

Epigenomic stability

Genome editing and aging

Studying how mutations, structural variation, CRISPR-induced breaks, and methylation disruption shape genome and epigenome stability in human cells.

Featured Work · 代表项目

Selected projects with translational and technical depth

01

Med, Cell Press

NanoRanger: rapid single base-pair resolution of genomic disorders

Led development of a nanopore sequencing diagnostic technology that improves breakpoint detection and carrier screening for complex genomic disorders. The work led to a PCT patent application and broad media attention from KAUST Research, GenomeWeb, China Daily, Science and Technology Daily, Saudi Press Agency, and Al Ekhbariya.

  • Targeted amplification and long-read analysis for clinically relevant genomic regions.
  • Designed to shorten the path from molecular suspicion to actionable breakpoint evidence.
02

Genome Biology

CRISPR-Cas9 breaks disrupt maintenance of epigenetic information

Work using long-read sequencing and methylation-aware analysis to show that genome editing can affect more than DNA sequence, revealing epigenetic consequences after double-strand breaks.

  • Integrated targeted native long-read sequencing with epigenetic readout.
  • Connected genome editing outcomes with methylation alteration and cellular-state questions.
  • Follow-up work appeared in BMC Research Notes in 2026.
03

Scientific Reports

Automated nanopore platform for COVID-19 diagnosis and surveillance

Helped create an open-source, automated, cost-effective platform for COVID-19 diagnosis and rapid portable genomic surveillance using nanopore sequencing, demonstrating scalable response capacity during public health pressure.

  • Combined automation, sequencing, and bioinformatic analysis.
  • Presented through KAUST Smart Health Initiative rapid-response showcase.
  • Built a foundation for later high-throughput diagnostic workflow development.
